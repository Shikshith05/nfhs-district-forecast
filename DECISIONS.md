## Cleaning

**D05. Value cleaning (NFHS-6).** `*` → NaN (suppressed: fewer than 25
unweighted cases). Brackets/parentheses, `%` and thousands separators are
stripped before converting to float; anything else unparseable → NaN. The
current file contains no bracketed values, but the cleaner handles them.
(`clean.clean_value`)

**D06. Excluded `Dadra & Nagar Haveli And Daman & Diu` from NFHS-6** (380 rows:
the state sheet plus three districts under one name, so rows cannot be assigned
to districts). Config: `cleaning.excluded_states_nfhs6`.

**D07. Excluded the India.csv placeholder `Jammu & Kashmir | Data Not
Available`** (104 rows, census key `_99`). Config:
`cleaning.excluded_districts_nfhs45`.

**D08. India.csv zeros are reclassified.** India.csv has no empty cells: its
parser wrote `na` (not available) and `*` (suppressed) as `0`. Evidence: of
the 2,394 cells that are `*` in the NFHS-6 file's own NFHS-5 column, 2,386 are
`0` in India.csv. Rules, in order (config `cleaning`):
1. a district-round with ≥ 80% zeros is *not available* (132 district-rounds,
   all NFHS-4: districts whose boundaries changed between NFHS-4 and NFHS-5);
2. an indicator-round with ≥ 95% zeros was *not measured* in that round
   (e.g. tobacco, alcohol, blood sugar, blood pressure, rotavirus and MCV2
   have no NFHS-4 comparison in the NFHS-5 fact sheets);
3. any other `0` is kept as a true zero only if at least 1% of that
   indicator-round's non-zero values lie in (0, 1), i.e. near-zero values are
   plausible (male sterilisation, injectables); otherwise it is treated as
   suppressed (e.g. 0% of children with diarrhoea receiving ORS).
Rule 3 is a heuristic: it may drop a few genuine zeros and keep a few
suppressed cells. The audit shows 809 India.csv zeros that are `0.0` in the
NFHS-6 file too.

**D09. India.csv source fixes.** Names are whitespace-trimmed
(`Chhattisgarh ` vs `Chhattisgarh`). The `Jharkhand | Sahibganj` name holds a
second full block keyed `Saraikela-kharsawan_24`; it is renamed to
Saraikela-Kharsawan (general rule: a full-size block whose `DISTRICT` key does
not resemble the district name takes the key's name). `Chhattisgarh | Surajpur`
has two different values for men's alcohol use in NFHS-5; both are set to
missing. `district_code` is the Census 2011 `censuscode` from the GeoJSON,
joined on the district's modal `DISTRICT` key. Split districts therefore share
their parent's code, and districts created after 2011 have none.

**D10. Indicators are matched by text only, never by `indicator_no`.**
Chandigarh and Lakshadweep come from the NFHS-6 *state* sheet, where numbering
differs (e.g. 18 = total fertility rate). Text normalisation: lowercase;
`≥`/`<` → words; drop `(%)`; drop footnote digits glued to a word or `)`
(`tetanus4`, `diet10, 11`, `kg/m2)15`); join hyphenated words (India.csv lost
its hyphens: `2024` for `20-24`, `drinkingwater`); strip punctuation; collapse
whitespace; drop trailing 1-2 digit footnotes (`tetanus 4`). Matching uses the
space-free form. Within a source: identical keys merge. A text that lost its
`(%)` ending (truncated) maps to the unique longer text it prefixes. A
minority variant maps to a majority variant at fuzz.ratio ≥ 97. Across
sources: exact, then prefix (≥ 25 chars), then fuzzy (≥ 90), one-to-one.
Two guards came from errors seen in the data:
- **Only texts without `(%)` count as truncated.** "Children under age 6
  months exclusively breastfed" is a real prefix of a *different* NFHS-6
  indicator.
- **Fuzzy matches must agree on population words** (women/men,
  public/private, …). Before this guard, "Men whose BMI…" was merged into
  "Women whose BMI…".

A sex qualifier from the category is applied only to the blood-sugar and
blood-pressure texts, which repeat verbatim for women and men. The NFHS-6
parser carries categories over wrongly (menstrual hygiene sits under `Men`),
so category is not used otherwise. Result: 67 exact, 4 prefix and 1 fuzzy
cross-round matches (see `data/processed/indicator_variants.csv`).

## Districts

**D11. District name matching.** Names are normalised (case, `&`→`and`,
punctuation, spacing), compared within state, then matched in order: exact,
exact ignoring spaces (`Bara Banki`/`Barabanki`), then an explicit alias table
of 55 renames and spellings in `config.yaml` (e.g. Gurugram→Gurgaon,
Nuh→Mewat, Ayodhya→Faizabad, Kalaburagi→Gulbarga, Hanumakonda→Warangal Urban).
Fuzzy matching (WRatio ≥ 90 with a 3-point margin) is a last resort but was
not needed. The NFHS-5 audit confirms the aliases: 95–99% agreement for every
alias district, with two exceptions. Maharajganj fails (the swap in D12).
Kanchipuram and Villupuram cannot be checked: they are boundary-flagged, so
the NFHS-6 file has no NFHS-5 values for them. See `district_name_map.csv`. The 17 unmatched NFHS-6 districts are
all new districts with `boundary_change_single_round == True`. Display names
are the NFHS-6 names; the key is `district_uid = "<state>|<district>"`.

**D12. Audit-based exclusions (NFHS-5 values only, so no leakage).** The
NFHS-6 file repeats NFHS-5 values. Comparing them with India.csv found two
kinds of problem, now handled by rules:
- **Indicators** whose NFHS-5 values agree (within 0.5) in fewer than 90% of
  districts are excluded (`indicators.min_audit_agreement`). This removed
  `health_insurance` (52% agreement; the NFHS-6 sheets restate NFHS-5 several
  points higher in some states, suggesting recalculation or redefinition).
  Children's second MCV dose also fails (0.3%) but was already out.
- **Districts** whose NFHS-5 values agree in fewer than 95% of the core
  indicators that pass the indicator rule are excluded
  (`matching.audit_min_district_agreement`). This removed **Maharajganj and
  Mahoba** (Uttar Pradesh), whose values are *swapped* between the two sources
  (verified value by value; which source is wrong is unknown). It also removed
  **Chandigarh and Lakshadweep**, where India.csv shows implausible values for
  child marriage, teen pregnancy and menstrual hygiene (e.g. 91.9% teen
  pregnancy), consistent with misaligned rows. The district rule uses core
  indicators only, so Dhule is kept: its misalignment is confined to
  tobacco/alcohol rows that are not modelled.

**D13. Core districts = 547.** A district is kept only if it is matched in all
three rounds, has `boundary_change_single_round == False`, has NFHS-4 and
NFHS-5 values, and passes the audit (D12). No harmonisation was attempted
(`nfhs_forecast/harmonise.py` is a documented stub). Excluded: 127 with no
NFHS-4 values, 17 new NFHS-6 districts, 16 matched but boundary-flagged, 13 not
in NFHS-6 (Manipur ×9, Puducherry district, Dadra & Nagar Haveli, Daman, Diu),
4 by audit. Districts without NFHS-4 could still be scored on NFHS-5 → NFHS-6
later; they are left out now to keep one population across both periods.

## Indicators

**D14. Indicator selection: 23 included out of 46 comparable.** 46 indicators
pass every comparability rule: a high/medium-confidence text match, no
definition flag, a percentage, ≥ 90% coverage in core districts in each of
NFHS-4 and NFHS-5, and NFHS-5 audit agreement ≥ 90%. The brief and one-pager
target 20–25 indicators, so a **core set** of 24 was fixed in `config.yaml`
(`indicators.core`). It covers WASH, women's empowerment and family planning,
maternal care, child health/vaccination and nutrition, with one indicator per
concept, preferring broad denominators and avoiding near-saturated or
redundant indicators (e.g. electricity, iodised salt, BCG, institutional
births in public facilities). `health_insurance` then failed the audit (D12),
leaving **23**. The other 23 comparable indicators are listed in the codebook
with `include = False` and can be switched on by adding them to the core list.
Coverage and the audit use rounds 4–5 only; NFHS-6 suppression rates were
deliberately *not* used to choose indicators. Note that improved sanitation
and clean cooking fuel are not in the NFHS-6 district sheets, so WASH is thin
(drinking water, menstrual hygiene).

**D15. Definition-based exclusions** (`indicators.definition_exclusions`):
- 3 doses of *penta or DPT* (NFHS-4/5) vs *pentavalent* (NFHS-6): different
  numerators.
- The three "adequate diet" indicators: NFHS-6 adds footnote 11, possibly the
  WHO 2021 IYCF revision. This is unverified, so they are excluded as
  ambiguous; check the fact-sheet footnotes before including them.

Not matched at all, correctly: hepatitis B (penta-or-HepB 3 doses vs birth
dose), pre-primary school (age 5 in 2019-20 vs age 2-4), third-order births vs
"three or more births", and anaemia (not measured in NFHS-6).

## Noise

**D16. Assumed standard errors (ASSUMPTIONS).** The fact sheets publish no
sample sizes. `SE = 100·sqrt(p(1−p)/n·deff)`, with p clipped to
[0.5/n, 1−0.5/n]. The design effect is 2.0, with a sensitivity range of
1.5–3.0 (`outputs/sensitivity_deff.csv`). The per-district n values are
assumptions derived from NFHS-5 national totals (636,699 households; 724,115
women; 101,839 men) over 707 districts, plus age structure. They extend the
four requested groups (households / women / men / children) to seven, because
denominators differ by an order of magnitude within a group:

| group | assumed n | used for |
|---|---|---|
| households | 900 | household / population indicators |
| women | 1000 | all women 15-49, currently married women, BMI |
| women_subgroup | 200 | women 20-24, 15-19, 15-24 |
| mothers | 250 | last birth / births in the last 5 years |
| men | 140 | men (not used by included indicators) |
| children | 300 | children under 5 (anthropometry, diarrhoea), under 3 |
| children_subgroup | 70 | children 12-23 months (vaccination), 9-35 months |

`small_sample_flag` rows have n capped at 37 (the midpoint of the 25–49
unweighted cases that fact sheets bracket), which inflates the SE. The flag
exists only in the NFHS-6 file and is per row (it does not say which round),
so it is applied to NFHS-6 values only. Using it for NFHS-5 SEs would carry
NFHS-6 information into training.

## Models and evaluation

**D17. Baselines** (predict t+1 from t, per indicator):
- *persistence*: y_t.
- *state trend*: y_t + mean change of the district's state, estimated on
  training pairs. In leave-one-state-out (LOSO) CV the held-out state is
  unseen, so it falls back to the national mean change of the training states
  (i.e. it is a national trend in CV). For NFHS-6 it uses each state's
  NFHS-4→5 change (extrapolation). The damping multiplier is 1.0
  (`models.state_trend_damping`).
- *state trend (oracle)*: the held-out state's own mean change from its
  other districts, using target-round values. It is reported as a reference
  for what knowing state-level change would buy (state fact sheets are
  published before district ones), flagged `honest = False`, kept out of the
  plot, and not computed for NFHS-6.
- *regression to the mean*: pooled OLS y_{t+1} = a + b·y_t on training pairs.
- *EB shrinkage*: y_t shrunk toward its state mean with weight
  B = τ²/(τ²+SE²), τ² pooled by method of moments (floored at 5% of the
  within-state variance). This is denoised persistence: it does not add a
  trend, and it uses only round-t data, so it is computed on the full round-t
  cross-section.

**D18. ML models.** One model per indicator, predicting the **change**
y_{t+1} − y_t, so regularisation shrinks toward persistence plus mean change
rather than toward a constant. Features: previous-round values of all 23
included indicators, the target indicator's state mean, one-hot state, and
one-hot region. Region (an approximate zonal grouping in `config.yaml`) is
added because a held-out state's own one-hot carries no information in LOSO.
- *Elastic net*: median imputation, scaling, and `ElasticNetCV` with an inner
  GroupKFold by state (alpha and l1_ratio tuned on NFHS-4→5 training folds
  only).
- *LightGBM*: fixed small configuration (150 trees, 7 leaves, depth 3,
  min_child_samples 25, λ = 10, subsampling); not tuned.

Predictions are clipped to [0, 100].

**D19. Evaluation.** LOSO CV over the 30 states/UTs with core districts.
Metrics are computed on rows where every method predicts and the truth exists:
MAE, RMSE, and skill = 1 − MSE/MSE_persistence, per indicator and OVERALL. The
OVERALL row pools squared errors across indicators, so higher-variance
indicators weigh more. CIs are 95%, from a state-clustered bootstrap (1,000
resamples of states). Also reported:
- *noise_mse*: the mean assumed SE² of the target observation, i.e. the error
  a perfect forecaster of the *true* value would still incur;
- *skill_ceiling*: 1 − noise_mse/MSE_persistence.

**D20. No-leakage controls.**
- `models.assert_no_round6` runs in every training entry point
  (`build_transition` for training, `fit_methods`, `loso_cv`, `freeze`,
  `training_data_hash`), and `run_pipeline.py` drops round 6 before step 8.
  Tests cover each.
- Indicator and district selection use NFHS-6 *metadata* (presence of the
  district and indicator, boundary flag) but never NFHS-6 *values*.
- `scripts/evaluate_nfhs6.py` was written but **not run**. It refuses to run
  without `--confirm-frozen` and aborts if the NFHS-4/5 training data hash
  differs from the frozen one. Predictions are made from a copy of the data
  without round-6 matrices; a test checks that changing round-6 values leaves
  every prediction unchanged.

**D21. Frozen models are committed** (`outputs/models/frozen_models.pkl`,
3 MB, plus `manifest.json` with training-data and config hashes). *Why:* the
team can tag an exact artifact. A re-run of `run_pipeline.py` reproduces the
same training hash, and the models are seeded.

**D22. Plot.** `outputs/skill_by_indicator.png` uses small multiples (one panel
per method) instead of five overlaid colours: identity comes from position,
not colour. A filled dot means the 95% CI excludes 0.
